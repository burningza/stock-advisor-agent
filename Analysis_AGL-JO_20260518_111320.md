# 📊 COMPREHENSIVE ANALYSIS OF ANGLO AMERICAN PLC (AGL.JO)
**As of 18 May 2026 | All currency in South African Rands (ZAR) unless otherwise stated**

---

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Anglo American plc (JSE: AGL.JO, LSE: AAL) is undergoing the most significant corporate transformation in its 109-year history. The company is executing a radical portfolio simplification, shedding coal, nickel, platinum, and diamonds to become a focused copper, premium iron ore, manganese, and crop nutrients producer. The landmark sale of Australian steelmaking coal to Dhilmar Ltd for up to R64.7 billion was announced today (18 May 2026), while the transformational merger with Teck Resources (valued at ~R900 billion) is on track for completion between September 2026 and March 2027.

The stock has rallied strongly, trading at **84,667 ZAc (R846.67)** – near the top of its 52-week range and well above both its 50-day (76,987 ZAc) and 200-day (68,259 ZAc) moving averages. Market capitalisation stands at **R907.2 billion**. Despite reported net losses driven by massive impairment charges (De Beers write-downs, discontinued operations), the underlying business is stabilising: revenue grew 17.5% YoY (current snapshot), EBITDA margins recovered to 32.2%, and operating cash flow is strongly positive at R5.5 billion.

The primary driver for the stock's re-rating is copper. With copper prices surging 35.7% year-on-year to $6.25/lb ($13,780/tonne) and the Teck merger creating a top-5 global copper producer with >70% copper exposure, Anglo American is uniquely leveraged to the electrification and energy transition megatrend. However, significant risks remain: negative free cash flow (-R591 million), ongoing De Beers separation uncertainty, regulatory hurdles (EU nickel review, South African Mining Charter), and dilution from the merger.

## 2. COMPANY OVERVIEW & STRATEGIC POSITIONING

### 2.1 Corporate Structure (Post-Restructuring)

Anglo American plc is a London-headquartered diversified mining company with primary listing on the London Stock Exchange and a secondary listing on the Johannesburg Stock Exchange (AGL.JO). It employs approximately 26,400 people.

**Current Portfolio (Continuing Operations):**
| Business | Key Assets | Location | Strategic Importance |
|----------|-----------|----------|---------------------|
| Copper | Los Bronces, Collahuasi (Chile); Quellaveco (Peru) | South America | **Core growth engine** – 70% of future portfolio |
| Premium Iron Ore | Kumba Iron Ore (Sishen, Kolomela) | South Africa | **Keep** – high-margin, low-cost |
| Manganese | Hotazel (SA), Groote Eylandt (Australia) | SA/Australia | **Keep** – battery metals exposure |
| Crop Nutrients | Woodsmith polyhalite mine | UK | **Keep** – long-term growth project |

**Discontinued Operations / Divestitures:**
| Business | Status | Detail |
|----------|--------|--------|
| Steelmaking Coal (Australia) | **SOLD 18 May 2026** to Dhilmar Ltd | Up to $3.88B (R64.7B) |
| Nickel (Brazil) | Sale to MMG – under EU regulatory review | Deadline 30 June 2026 |
| De Beers (Diamonds) | Separation in progress – impairment taken | Demerger or sale pending |
| Valterra Platinum (ex-AMP) | **Demerged May 2025** – residual sold Sep 2025 | Now independent JSE-listed |

### 2.2 Transformation Rationale

The strategy, initiated in 2024 under CEO Duncan Wanblad, is to create a **simpler, higher-margin, copper-led business**. The rationale is compelling:
- Copper is the metal of electrification – demand is structurally supported by EV adoption, grid expansion, and renewable energy infrastructure.
- Anglo's legacy portfolio was overly complex, with exposure to structurally declining or cyclical commodities (coal, PGMs, diamonds) that weighed on valuation.
- The Teck merger doubles copper production and provides access to world-class growth projects (Quebrada Blanca Phase 2, Highland Valley Copper).
- Portfolio simplification reduces operational risk and improves capital allocation focus.

## 3. FINANCIAL HEALTH ANALYSIS (in ZAR)

**Key Observations:**

1. **Revenue collapse and recovery:** Total revenue fell by ~50% from FY2022 to FY2024 (R35.1B → R17.7B), driven by weaker commodity prices (especially PGMs and coal) and asset sales. The **17.5% YoY growth** in the current snapshot (FY2025 annual +4.52%) signals a **bottoming-out**, supported by stronger copper and iron ore prices.

2. **Reported losses mask underlying recovery:** Reported net income has been negative for two consecutive years (FY2024: -R2.79B, FY2025: -R3.17B) due to **massive impairment charges** – R4.9B in FY2024 and R2.28B in FY2025 (mainly De Beers write-downs and discontinued operations losses of R2.47B). However, **normalized net income turned positive** in FY2025 (R705 million vs R427M in FY2024), indicating the operating businesses are generating profits.

3. **Margins are structurally healthy:**
   - **Gross margin remained strong** at 51-55% across all periods, demonstrating that the underlying mining operations have good product margins even in a weaker pricing environment.
   - **EBITDA margin collapsed to 9.07% in FY2024** but **recovered sharply to 21.82% in FY2025** (32.22% per current snapshot), reflecting cost controls, operational leverage, and higher copper contributions.
   - **Net profit margin remains negative (-20.17% in FY2025)** purely due to non-cash impairments – normalized net margin was +3.8%.

4. **Operating income turned positive again** in FY2025 (R1.75B after being -R578M in FY2024), a 402% improvement. This is the most important operating metric.

## 6. NEWS & CATALYSTS INTEGRATION

### 6.1 Breaking: Sale of Australian Steelmaking Coal to Dhilmar (18 May 2026)

**Deal Terms:**
- **Consideration:** Up to US$3.875 billion (R64.7 billion) in cash
- **Upfront:** $2.05 billion on closing
- **Deferred payments:** Staged over time
- **Conditions:** Competition approvals

**Impact:**
- **Removes coal exposure** – completes Anglo's exit from thermal and steelmaking coal, aligning with ESG-focused investors.
- **Generates significant cash** – R64.7 billion (if fully received) can be used to reduce debt (current net debt estimated at R150-200B) or fund Woodsmith capex.
- **Simplifies portfolio** – management can now focus fully on copper, iron ore, and crop nutrients.
- **Mitigates execution risk** – after Peabody walked away using a MAC clause (fire at Moranbah North), Dhilmar's offer shows asset quality is still high.

**Potential Concern:** Dhilmar Limited is a UK-based mining company with limited track record. There is risk of financing failure or regulatory block. However, the upfront payment structure reduces Anglo's exposure.

### 6.2 Anglo-Tech Resources Merger: "Anglo Teck" on Track

| Detail | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Deal Value | ~$53 billion (R900 billion) |
| Structure | Merger of equals |
| Shareholder Approval | Expected by September 2025 (already received) |
| Regulatory Approvals | Canadian government granted approval (May 2026) |
| Completion Timeline | September 2026 – March 2027 |
| Combined Copper Production | ~1.2 Mt/year (top-5 globally) |
| New Entity Name | Anglo Teck |
| Listing | Likely LSE + TSX |

**Strategic Benefits:**
- Creates a **pure-play copper giant** with world-class assets in Chile, Peru, and Canada.
- **Combined reserves and resources** exceed 50 million tonnes of copper – decades of production.
- **Synergies** of $500-800 million annually (corporate overhead, procurement, technology).
- **Diversified geographic risk** – Chile, Peru, Canada (low political risk vs Chile alone).
- **Growth pipeline** – Quebrada Blanca Phase 3, Highland Valley expansion, Resolution project (Arizona).

**Risks:**
- **Execution risk** – integrating two large organisations is complex.
- **Dilution** – existing Anglo shareholders will own ~55% of combined entity; Teck shareholders ~45%.
- **Copper dependence** – the new company will have >70% exposure to copper, making it highly sensitive to copper price cycles.
- **Canada regulatory conditions** – may include Canadian ownership requirements, board representation, or operational commitments.

### 6.3 De Beers Impairment & Separation Impasse (14 May 2026)

Anglo announced an **additional write-down** of De Beers' value amid weak diamond demand. Key points:
- Diamond production being reduced in response to market conditions.
- The write-down complicates the planned separation (sale or demerger).
- CEO stated separation is "progressing" but no specific date provided.
- A demerger would list De Beers separately – valuation likely lower than anticipated.

**Impact on Anglo Stock:**
- Negative for near-term sentiment – shows ongoing impairment risk.
- Positive if it accelerates management's willingness to accept a lower price and move on.
- The impairment is a **non-cash charge** but reduces book value and may impact covenant compliance with lenders.

### 6.4 Nickel Sale to MMG – EU Regulatory Delay

- The sale of the Brazilian nickel business (Barro Alto, Codemin) to MMG for up to $500 million is under **Phase II EU competition investigation**.
- New deadline: **30 June 2026**.
- A competing bid of **$400 million** from Robert Yuksel Yildirim's group has emerged.
- If the MMG deal fails, Anglo may need to accept a lower price or retain the assets.

**Impact:** Minor for the overall thesis – nickel is a small part of the portfolio. However, a failed sale would delay the simplification timeline and tie up management attention.

### 6.5 South African Regulatory Landscape

**a) Mineral Resources Development Bill (MRRDB):**
- Draft bill progressing through parliament.
- Strengthens B-BBEE ownership requirements, community obligations, and state participation in strategic minerals.
- Likely to increase compliance costs for Kumba Iron Ore and manganese operations.
- Anglo has strong B-BBEE credentials (Level 1), but the bill may impose additional conditions on mining right renewals.

**b) Private Rail Access:**
- South Africa has officially allowed 11 private companies to operate trains on Transnet's freight network.
- **Game-changer** for Kumba Iron Ore – logistics have been a major bottleneck (moving ore to Saldanha Bay).
- Kumba can now invest in its own rolling stock or contract with third-party operators, potentially reducing costs and increasing capacity.
- Positive for Anglo's South African operations.

**c) Energy Security:**
- Eskom load-shedding has reduced in 2026 but remains a risk.
- Anglo has invested in solar and wind farms at its South African mines, providing energy security and cost certainty.
- Carbon tax increases are manageable given Anglo's renewable energy shift.

### 6.6 Other Notable Events

- **Valterra Platinum demerger completed** (May 2025) – residual holding sold September 2025. Clean exit.
- **Woodsmith polyhalite project** continues – capex heavy but long-term optionality on crop nutrients.
- **Share buyback?** With the coal sale proceeds, Anglo may consider a buyback or debt reduction. The company has not announced a dividend reinstatement yet.
